2 · LANDLORD-TENANT CODE

The code in 60 seconds

RENTING IN HAWAIʻI RUNS ON ONE LAW: HRS Chapter 521, the Landlord-Tenant Code. It sets the rules for deposits, repairs, privacy, rent hikes, and evictions — but it was written by lawyers, for lawyers. This guide translates it into plain language, so you know exactly what you're owed and what you owe.

HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE. Find your situation, read the section, and note the § numbers — they point to the exact law, so you can quote it in a letter, a complaint, or in court. A reference like §521-44(b) means: chapter 521, section 44, subsection (b). Look any of them up free at capitol.hawaii.gov.

PART I · THE GUIDE

Rental agreements

NO PAPER? STILL A LEASE — TECHNICALLY. A spoken agreement creates a real month-to-month tenancy (§521-22), but spoken terms are hard to prove. Put everything in writing — and remember that any new or changed term needs both parties' written agreement.

MONTH-TO-MONTH · §521-22

  • The default when no fixed term is set
  • Either side may end it with proper written notice — 45 days from your landlord
  • No changes to rent or rules without legally required written notice

FIXED-TERM LEASE · §521-71

  • Ends on its end date — no extra notice needed
  • Stay past it (or the landlord accepts rent)? You're month-to-month now
  • Oral agreements can't run longer than one year

VOID — EVEN IF YOU SIGNED IT (§521-31 to 35). A lease can't take away rights the law gives you. Clauses waiving your right to a livable home, to sue, or to proper notice — or excusing the landlord's own negligence — are unenforceable. Full stop.

Move-in math §521-44(b)

ALL A LANDLORD MAY COLLECT:

✓ First month's rent

✓ Security deposit

That's the whole list.

NOT ALLOWED UP FRONT:

✗ Last month's rent

✗ "Admin" or "processing" fees

✗ Anything else — you can recover unlawful charges in court.

SCREENING FEES (§521-46) may only cover the actual cost of checking your application. Anything beyond that must be returned within 30 days — and you can ask for an itemized receipt.

PART I · THE GUIDE

Security deposits

14

DAYS to return your deposit after you move out and hand back the keys — no exceptions, no extensions. §521-44(c)

THE CAP: one month's rent, plus up to one more as a pet deposit. Service and assistance animals are not pets (§515-3) — no pet deposit allowed for them.

THE PAPER TRAIL: any money kept must come with a written itemized list plus receipts or invoices. A vague "damages" line is not legally sufficient, and a lump-sum deduction is invalid.

FAIR GAME TO DEDUCT · §521-44(a)

✓ Unpaid rent

✓ Cleaning, if you left it dirtier than you got it

✓ Damage beyond normal wear

✓ Unreturned keys or fobs

OFF-LIMITS

✗ Faded paint

✗ Worn carpet

✗ Minor scuffs

Normal wear and tear is the landlord's cost of doing business.

MISS THE DEADLINE, LOSE IT ALL. A landlord who blows the 14-day deadline forfeits the right to keep any of it. Withheld deliberately, with no valid claim? Courts can award you up to 3× the amount, plus court costs.

BUILDING SOLD? (§521-44(f)) Your lease and your deposit ride along: the new owner is bound by both. They owe you written notice of your deposit amount within 20 days — or the law assumes it equals one month's rent at your original rate.

PART I · THE GUIDE

Rent & fees

RENT IS DUE at the start of each rental period — usually the 1st — unless your lease says otherwise (§521-21(b)). It can't be demanded early, and it's pro-rated by the day if your tenancy starts mid-period.

Raising the rent: the notice clock §521-21(d)(e)

Month-to-month

45 days' notice

Week-to-week

15 days' notice

THE 60-DAY RED FLAG (§521-74). A rent hike within 60 days of your complaint or repair request is presumed retaliatory — in court, the burden is on the landlord to prove it wasn't. Even a threatened raise in response to a maintenance request can violate the law.

LATE FEES · §521-21

  • Only enforceable if the lease explicitly allows them
  • Must be reasonable
  • Lease silent? Then generally no late fee at all
  • There's no statewide grace period — know your due date

POSTDATED CHECKS · §521-44(e)

A landlord cannot require postdated checks or other pre-signed payments for future rent. If they do, you have grounds for a legal remedy.

6 · LANDLORD-TENANT CODE

4·5

PART I · THE GUIDE

Who owes what

YOUR LANDLORD MUST · §521-42, 43, 57

  • Keep the place livable: weatherproofed, working plumbing, hot and cold water, safe electrical, smoke detectors, no pests
  • Act within a reasonable time once you report a problem in writing — and never bill you for repairs that are their duty
  • Disclose in writing who owns the building and who takes notices — skipping it gives you a $100 claim
  • Keep water, power, and gas on — always

YOU MUST · §521-51, 52

  • Pay rent when due — after a written pay-or-quit notice, nonpayment can end in eviction
  • Keep the unit reasonably clean and dispose of trash properly
  • Not damage the place beyond normal wear and tear
  • Follow the lease on subletting (§521-31): if consent is required it can't be unreasonably refused; if subletting is banned outright, that ban sticks

UTILITY SHUTOFF = ILLEGAL EVICTION (§521-57). Cutting water, power, or gas to squeeze a tenant out is unlawful even if rent is owed. Penalty: 3× monthly rent or $1,000, whichever is greater, plus other damages. Only a court order can remove a tenant.

LANDLORD WON'T FIX IT? Put it in writing, in this order:

  1. The landlord — a written report starts their legal duty to act
  2. Still nothing? Escalate: Department of Health (health & sanitation), your county planning or housing department (building code), or the Office of Consumer Protection (general complaints)

One more: a landlord can't charge rent for any period the unit was uninhabitable because they failed to maintain it (§521-51).

PART I · THE GUIDE

Privacy & moving on

2 DAYS

That's the written notice your landlord owes you before entering — for inspections, repairs, or showings — and only at reasonable hours, roughly 8am–8pm (§521-53). No notice? You may refuse entry, except in a genuine emergency. You can waive the notice in writing for a single visit, and entry rights may never be used to harass you.

Ending a tenancy: who gives how much notice

YOU end month-to-month · §521-71

28 days

LANDLORD ends month-to-month · §521-71

45 days

Condo conversion · §521-38

120 days

Even after a notice period runs out, only a court order removes a tenant. And if the unit becomes uninhabitable, you may end the tenancy early (§521-61) — with no penalty for doing so.

"ABANDONED" HAS A DEFINITION · §521-70

Gone 20+ consecutive days and no rent paid and words or actions saying you're not coming back. A paid-up tenant on a long trip has abandoned nothing — but check your lease: it may require notice of long absences.

LEASE RAN OUT, STILL THERE?

A fixed-term lease just ends on its date (§521-71). Stay on without a new agreement — or have rent accepted — and you've got a month-to-month tenancy under the same rules as before.

PART I · THE GUIDE

Eviction: the only legal path

NO JUDGE, NO EVICTION (§521-68 to 74). Every lawful eviction in Hawaiʻi runs through this pipeline. A landlord who skips ahead is breaking the law — no matter what the notice on your door says.

1 · WRITTEN NOTICE The clock starts: pay, fix, or move by the deadline.

2 · MEDIATION WINDOW (nonpayment cases · §521-68) A copy of the notice goes to Mediation Centers of Hawaiʻi automatically. You have 10 days to opt in — it's free, and if you do, the landlord must participate.

3 · LANDLORD FILES IN COURT Not before the notice period runs out.

4 · HEARING A judge hears both sides and decides.

5 · SHERIFF ENFORCES THE ORDER Only a sheriff with a court order can remove you.

NEVER LEGAL — EVICTION OR NOT:

✗ Changing your locks

✗ Removing doors or windows

✗ Shutting off utilities

✗ Taking your appliances

✗ Threats & intimidation

These are illegal "self-help evictions," and they expose a landlord to significant damages.

NEW & TEMPORARY: the mediation step is a pilot program (Act 278 of 2025), running through Feb 4, 2028.

mediationcentersofhawaii.org

If you request mediation and reach a deal, it goes in writing, signed by both sides. Request it and skip the session, though, and the landlord can seek their attorney's fees.

PART I · THE GUIDE — REMEDIES

Fight back, by the book

THE CODE GIVES YOU TOOLS — EACH WITH ITS OWN RULEBOOK. Used right, they're powerful. Used loosely, they can backfire into an eviction case. Here's how to use each one safely.

Repair & deduct §521-64, 65

STEP 1 Written notice of the problem to your landlord.

STEP 2 A government agency cites the health or safety violation.

THEN: if the landlord still does nothing for 1 week, you may hire the repair and deduct the cost from rent — up to one month's rent per repair, receipts required. Skip either step first and the remedy collapses — you could face eviction instead.

RENT WITHHOLDING · §521-78

If the unit is legally uninhabitable through the landlord's failure, you may withhold rent or terminate after proper written notice. Talk to the Landlord-Tenant Information Center or an attorney first — a misstep here can mean eviction.

SMALL CLAIMS COURT · HRS 633

Disputes up to $10,000 — or any amount for security deposits. In deposit-only cases neither side may bring an attorney, which levels the field. Landlords and tenants both use it: unpaid rent, damages, wrongly kept deposits.

AND REMEMBER RETALIATION (§521-74): eviction or a rent hike for reporting violations, joining a tenants' union, or requesting required repairs is illegal — and presumed retaliatory within 60 days of any of those.

PART II

Where the code falls short

THE CODE ASSUMES YOU'LL ENFORCE IT YOURSELF. Chapter 521 is a real framework — but it's a self-help law. Nobody polices it. If your rights are violated, it falls to you, on your own, to:

1 · Know the rights the code gives you

2 · Assert them to the other party

3 · Find the right agency to complain to

4 · File in court — and prove your case

Who has a lawyer in eviction court?

TENANTS

fewer than 5%

LANDLORDS

more than 80%

Eviction cases are complex and intimidating, and unrepresented tenants start at a deep disadvantage. Many lose their homes not because they lack a defense — but because they lack the resources to use it.1 Outcomes track representation, not the merits.2

The no-cause loophole

Retaliatory eviction is illegal (§521-74) — but a landlord can end any month-to-month tenancy with a 45-day no-cause notice: no reason given, none required. A landlord who never has to state a reason never has to state that reason — which makes the anti-retaliation shield nearly unenforceable in practice.

AND THE STAKES ARE HIGH: eviction is linked to drug-use initiation, elevated blood pressure, weight gain, heart disease, and increased risk of domestic abuse.3 Meanwhile only about 4% of Hawaiʻi rentals sit vacant and available, and well over half of renters are cost-burdened4 — a tenant protected on paper, with nowhere else to go, still has no real choice.

PART III

What reform looks like

JUST CAUSE EVICTION (JCE) doesn't replace Chapter 521 — it plugs the holes. Before ending a tenancy, a landlord would need a legally recognized reason: at-fault (nonpayment, lease violations, property damage) or no-fault (owner move-in, major renovation — with evidence and 90 days' notice).5

End a tenancy for any reason — or none — on 45 days' notice

Every termination needs a stated, legally recognized cause

Anti-retaliation rules dodged with no-cause notices

Notices must cite the cause; pretextual or discriminatory evictions banned

No guaranteed chance to fix a violation

Cure periods: 14 days for nonpayment, 14–30 days for lease breaches

"I'm selling" — with no proof required

Must list the unit within 60 days, or owe a month's rent plus the tenant's new security deposit

Same rules for a spare-room landlord and a portfolio operator

Exempt: owner-occupied properties under 4 units; new construction for 10 years

Tenants face eviction court alone

Right to counsel in eviction cases, plus expanded eviction-defense legal aid

TODAY'S GAP

THE JCE FIX

REFORM, WITH EYES OPEN. Design matters — sloppy just-cause rules can chill construction and push costs onto tenants, which is exactly why the exemptions above exist.5 And just cause fixes the terms of displacement, not the shortage that makes it devastating: real tenant power arrives when landlords must compete for tenants.6 Protections set the floor. Supply shifts the balance.
